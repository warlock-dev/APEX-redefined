# Why the app works this way

These are the ideas behind the design. They are cited by name, not as proof that the app improves results.

- **Implementation intentions.** Planning "if this, then that" makes a chosen action more likely to happen. Gollwitzer and Sheeran's 2006 meta-analysis summarises this across many studies. The app's version is the "Now" card: one next action, not a full plan.
- **Defaults.** People tend to keep the option already set (Johnson and Goldstein, 2003). New tasks start at 25 minutes, and the day's verdict has one tap.
- **Goal gradient.** Progress toward a goal speeds up as the goal nears (Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng, 2006). The app shows "2 of 5 done" rather than a score.
- **Spaced review.** Returning to material after increasing gaps improves long-term recall. The app's ladder (1, 3, 7, 15, 30, 60 days) is a common practical schedule; the exact gaps are a default you can change in the code, not a proven optimum.
- **Fresh starts.** Planning moments such as a new week or month attract people to start new habits (Dai, Milkman and Riis, 2014). The app does not yet use this; it is the next thing to add.
- **No streaks.** Counters that reset after one missed day tend to discourage people who slip. The app shows the last seven days instead.
